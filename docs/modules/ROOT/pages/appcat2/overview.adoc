= Helmetica Overview

Helmetica turns one Helm chart into a service that users can order many instances of.
Three roles work with it, and each one touches different resources.

The names come from alchemy, see the xref:appcat2/servicemaintainer/glossary.adoc[glossary] for every term.

[#framework]
== The framework at a glance

The map shows where each piece lives, what it holds, and who it belongs to.
The <<maintainer,service maintainer>> works outside the cluster, the <<operator,framework operator>> runs the platform and decides which services are installed, and the <<user,user>> works in their own namespace.
The dashed regions are managed by the framework, nobody edits them by hand.
The bold arrows are the hand-overs between the roles.

A reagent is a Helm chart.
Besides the service itself it renders small, platform-agnostic resources such as `NetworkConfig` or `BackupPolicy`, and the framework turns each of them into the real thing.
Backup, network, maintenance and billing are switched on in the reagent's `values.yaml` and rendered by azoth.
Credentials are defined in the reagent's own `templates/credentials.yaml`.

[cols="1,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Role | Job | You work with

| <<operator,Framework operator>>
| Runs the framework on a cluster and provides the platform it relies on.
| Installed services, platform values, `MaintenanceWindow`, FluxCD, COSI driver, K8up, Gateways

| <<maintainer,Service maintainer>>
| Wraps an upstream chart and publishes it as a service.
| Reagent chart, `values.yaml`, rituals, credentials

| <<user,User>>
| Orders and operates instances of a service.
| Claim, `Action`, `ClaimRevision`
|===

[#operator]
== Framework operator

The operator keeps the framework and the platform it needs running.
They don't write charts or order services.

* *Installs the framework.* Its controllers and CRDs have to be installed on the cluster.
  Which controllers exist and what they own is documented in each project's repository.
* *Installs services.* Installing a published reagent, for example Redis, on a cluster is what makes it orderable.
  The installation applies the service's `ClaimDefinition`, one per major version, together with the FluxCD `OCIRepository` and `ImageRepository` it points at.
  These manifests ship with the reagent, so nobody writes them by hand.
  The framework then generates the CRD users order from.
* *Provides the platform.* The framework's resources need something to land on, see the diagram.
* *Describes the platform.* Config maps annotated with `chrysopoeia.io/platform-values` end up under `__platform__` in every instance's values, so charts can adapt to the distribution or load balancer capabilities.
  `MaintenanceWindow` objects define when maintenance may start for all instances.

[#maintainer]
== Service maintainer

The maintainer turns an upstream chart into a service and ships it.

. `transmuter transmute` scaffolds a reagent from ferment and adds the upstream chart (prima materia) as a dependency.
. Ferment brings the azoth library chart along, which renders the backup, network, maintenance, alerting, monitoring and billing resources from the reagent's `values.yaml`.
  Ferment also ships a `templates/credentials.yaml` to fill in with the service's connection details.
. You adjust the values, rituals and credentials, and try the reagent in athanor, a local development cluster.
. `transmuter assay` checks the chart and detects breaking CRD changes against the latest published version. Touchstone tests it end to end.
. You publish the chart to an OCI registry.
  That is the hand-over: from there, the operator installs the service on a cluster and the framework does the rest.

[#user]
== User

As a user you order an instance of a service and work with it, all from your own namespace.
The service itself runs in a separate namespace the framework creates for you, but you never have to go there.

Order:: You create a claim with the configuration and the approval strategy.
Setting `spec.version` pins a version.
Without it, a new instance starts on the newest version the CRD allows and only moves on through auto-updates.
The framework records it as a `ClaimRevision` next to the claim.
Once the revision is approved, it creates the instance namespace and installs the service there.
The claim's `status.instanceNamespace` tells you where.
Credentials:: The connection details are written to a secret beside your claim.
The service accepts connections from your namespace.
Change:: Every change to the claim creates a new revision.
So does a change to the platform values, without you touching the claim.
Unless the approval strategy is `Automatic`, nothing is deployed until someone approves it.
`cupel weigh` shows what a change would do, `cupel ledger` browses the history and approves.
Operate:: To run an operation such as a restart, create an `Action` that names your claim and the ritual.
Its status shows whether it succeeded.
